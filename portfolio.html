01 / 07Space systems · 2022-2025

Motion systems for environmental testing

Two ESA ESTEC mechanisms developed for moving payloads or instrumentation during thermal-vacuum testing: the VTC1.5 translation stage and COLIBRIS.

Role
Lead Design Engineer, VTC1.5; Principal Design Engineer, COLIBRIS
Engineering challenge
Create reliable, manufacturable motion hardware for demanding environmental-test facilities, then carry it through analysis, assembly and qualification.

My contribution

  • Progressed the VTC1.5 system from concept through subsystem design, integration and TVAC qualification; completed structural and thermoelastic assessment and supported test execution.
  • Took full design ownership of COLIBRIS, developing the mechanism from concept through critical design review and manufacturing in a compressed schedule.
  • Worked across mechanical design, CAD, analysis, interface definition, manufacturing documentation and environmental-test readiness.

Outcome

The VTC1.5 system completed its qualification campaign and repositioned a 50 kg test payload in vacuum at cryogenic temperature. The work was presented at ECSSMET; COLIBRIS was subsequently presented at the Aerospace Testing Seminar. Detailed agency-marked design imagery is intentionally not reproduced.

02 / 07Space systems · 2022-2024

Flight payload hardware: YPSAT and AstroBit

Mechanical design, integration and environmental-test work for two ESA payloads that reached space: YPSAT on Ariane 6 and AstroBit on the International Space Station.

Role
Structural-design contributor, YPSAT; Lead Design Engineer, AstroBit
Engineering challenge
Deliver compact payload hardware that could be assembled, tested and flown within strict spacecraft and launch-interface constraints.

My contribution

  • For YPSAT, co-led structural design and final component delivery; authored electro-mechanical assembly procedures and supported cleanroom integration.
  • Defined vibration test profiles, developed notching approaches and processed test data during qualification and acceptance campaigns.
  • For AstroBit, led mechanical concept development, design, build and test of a flight case for an educational micro:bit payload, maintaining access to user interfaces and power.

Outcome

YPSAT flew on Ariane 6's maiden flight in July 2024 and achieved its mission objectives. AstroBit was operated aboard the ISS during ESA's Huginn mission in 2023. Team contribution is distinguished from individual design ownership above.

03 / 07Propulsion · 2021-2023

Cranfield propulsion: C-Star, HYDRA and HYROE-1

A progression from student-led liquid-engine development to hybrid propulsion and test-stand design, combining mechanical systems, integration and practical test experience.

Role
Co-founder and project lead, C-Star; Lead Propulsion Engineer, HYDRA; Lead Propulsion Engineer, HYROE-1
Engineering challenge
Coordinate mechanical, controls, instrumentation and test infrastructure so an academic propulsion programme could progress from design to safe, instrumented testing.

My contribution

  • Co-founded and led Project C-Star, taking the 1.5 kN Cyclone bipropellant engine programme from concept toward a successful 2022 hot-fire.
  • Designed, built and tested supporting feed, control, ignition and data-acquisition systems, together with test-stand and tank-stand hardware.
  • Led the 0.3 kN HYDRA hybrid engine, which was successfully hot-fired at UK Race2Space; separately led design of the 350 N HYROE-1 engine and test stand through critical design review.

Outcome

C-Star achieved a successful hot-fire and met the programme's primary demonstration objective. The projects strengthened my ability to connect component design with instrumentation, assembly, test planning and evidence-led iteration. Detailed operating parameters and procedural material are intentionally excluded.

04 / 07Launch systems · 2021-2022

AETHER-I recoverable launch vehicle

Structural design of a proposed UK-based, recoverable two-stage microsatellite launch vehicle developed as a Cranfield group design project.

Role
Lead Structural Engineer within the AETHER-I design team
Engineering challenge
Develop a structurally credible vehicle concept around payload, launch-load and recovery requirements, while integrating the structures workstream with the wider vehicle design.

My contribution

  • Led the structural-design workstream and contributed to the vehicle-level design definition.
  • Developed structural sizing and assessment for the launcher design cases, including maximum-dynamic-pressure loading.
  • Balanced structural requirements with a low-complexity, transportable concept intended for UK launch-site operations.

Outcome

The academic design study proposed a launcher capable of placing up to 200 kg into a 500 km polar orbit. This was a conceptual vehicle design, not a built or flown launcher.

05 / 07Space systems · 2021-2022

Student spacecraft and launch payloads

Mechanical design contributions to a 3U CubeSat concept and a sounding-rocket payload, linking CAD definition with team integration and launch outcomes.

Role
Lead Mechanical Design Engineer, Edensat-7; Lead Payload Design Engineer, Mach-22
Engineering challenge
Turn mission and competition requirements into compact, buildable payload concepts within short, multidisciplinary student-project schedules.

My contribution

  • Designed and modelled the 3U Edensat-7 CubeSat concept during the UKSEDS Spaceport Cornwall 48-hour hackathon; the team was named a 2021 winner.

07 / 07University projects · 2017-2021

Cardiff University: mechanical design and systems modelling

Selected BEng work spanning a hybrid renewable-energy system study and component-design / FEA coursework.

Role
Individual thesis author for the renewable-energy study; undergraduate coursework for component design
Engineering challenge
Use engineering analysis to size and assess systems against real load and site conditions, while developing sound CAD, FEA and drawing practice.

My contribution

  • Developed a simulation model for a wind-solar-battery system intended to support water pumping and remote communications using an unfavourable urban renewable resource.
  • Analysed wind data, sized system components and evaluated simulated energy-supply reliability; physical validation and economic assessment were identified as future work.
  • Selected component-design coursework covered CAD, structural FEA, mesh sensitivity, tolerance selection and manufacturing drawings. Uploaded coursework images are presented as a broad visual sample because their assignment-level mapping is incomplete.

Outcome

The renewable-system model indicated that the proposed configuration could meet the modelled loads under the study assumptions; this was a simulation result, not a physical demonstration.
